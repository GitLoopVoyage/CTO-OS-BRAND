Assertion panel

Makes a claim about current state. Must carry an axis, an as-of and, where material, an evidence reference. The most constrained panel type.

PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED09:14Z

Register panel

Lists records — decisions, assumptions, authorities. Asserts existence and ordering, not truth. Rows carry their own axes; the panel asserts nothing on their behalf.

Working panel

Composition, drafting, simulation. May show projections. Must be visibly separable from assertion panels, because Art. 30.3 makes simulation not proof.

Nesting rule

A panel may never assert more than the strongest claim among its children. A register of four rows, one UNKNOWN, cannot summarise itself as SUPPORTED. Summarising a mixed set takes the weakest value, not the average and not the mode.

Density

Two modes. Comfortable for decision surfaces where a human is choosing; compact for state tables where a human is scanning. Density changes row height, padding and gap — never type colour, never state vocabulary, never what is shown. Nothing disappears when the view tightens.

Why three

Most products ship one empty state and it says something like “Nothing here yet.” That sentence collapses three different facts: we have not looked, you may not look, and it cannot be known. In a governance product those are the three most important distinctions on the screen, so they are three components, designed before anything that carries data.

Reference tenant is synthetic. Meridian Logistics is invented, as are its services, authorities, evidence records and incidents. Nothing here is a real organisation’s posture. The shell declares data-fixture-root="synthetic" and every fixture node is inside it.
